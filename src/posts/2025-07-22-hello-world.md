## the origins of hello world

When it comes to programming, I have noticed that some people have forgotten
-or simply never learned- the origins of why we always start
out with a `Hello World` program.
It doesn't matter if you code in C, Java, TypeScript, Rust or Swift.
The first program you learn is `Hello World`.
And this doesn't just apply to learning a new language.
When setting up projects in languages or frameworks I can navigate in my sleep,
I always start by creating something that displays
or responds with `Hello World` in some fashion.

The convention of the `Hello World` program goes back to 1978,
when Kernighan and Ritchie published their book _The C Programming Language_[^1].

There was an example program that looked like this:

```c
main( ){
    printf("hello, world");
}
```

And through that, they set one of the longest standing coding "conventions" to date.

## why i think it matters

While habits aren't a bad thing, I sometimes feel a bit lost in them;
especially when I forgot why I was doing them in the first place.
You do things the same way over and over again,
and at some point you don't even know why anymore.

In those situations I often take a second to reflect about the **why**.
It gives time to mull over if what you are doing is still "_the way to do it_".
Or, you could find some happiness through rediscovering the meaning of
a small task or habit in your life.

When you get lost in the daily routines created through your job, college,
university or school, remember that there is meaning even to the smallest things.
It might boil down to you just "_liking it better this way_" -
or a book that got published almost 50 years. And I think that's pretty cool.
