## forgetting isn't always loss

We often treat forgetting like failure - a misplaced memory, a fading detail,
a sign that something is slipping away. But forgetting is not always erosion.
Sometimes it is simply space being cleared. The mind, like a room constantly
lived in, cannot preserve every object in perfect order. And in that gentle
disorder lies an unexpected gift.

Forgetting softens the sharp edges of familiarity. It loosens the grip of
routine. What was once obvious can quietly become new again.

## beauty through rediscovery

Rediscovery carries a special kind of joy - one that novelty alone cannot replicate.
When we encounter something we once knew, there is both recognition and
surprise. A melody heard after years. A forgotten photograph. A place revisited.

It feels layered. Past and present briefly overlap. We are not just seeing the
thing itself; we are seeing who we were when we first met it.
Rediscovery becomes a conversation between versions of ourselves.

## breathing room for wonders

Constant remembrance can dull experience. When everything stays vividly accessible,
little has the chance to feel fresh. Forgetting, in its subtle way, restores contrast.
It allows wonder to return.

To forget is to make room for delight - for the small spark of “Oh, I remember this,”
which is never quite the same as seeing something for the first time, yet
somehow richer.

Perhaps beauty is not found only in holding on, but also in letting certain
things drift - trusting that some of them will find their way back,
carrying joy with them.
